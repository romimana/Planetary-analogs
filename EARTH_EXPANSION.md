# Earth expansion for the analog explorer

The browser app reads the original public snapshot and a separate `data/earth-expansion.sqlite` snapshot. The [coverage report](data/earth-expansion.json) counts candidates after chemistry and GIS terrain validation. Rows retain the survey, original sample ID, source file, SHA-256 and row locator.

- **United States.** Smith et al. (2013), [USGS Data Series 801](https://doi.org/10.3133/ds801), Appendix 2b. Use the 0–5 cm soil horizon, with positions supplied in WGS84. Convert reported magnesium, calcium, potassium, titanium and manganese to oxide-equivalent mass percentages. The source uses total or near-total ICP-based chemistry. Keep censored strings as missing. Silicon was unavailable in the table and silica remains missing. A quartz mineral percentage cannot supply total silica.
- **Ireland.** Geological Survey Ireland Tellus, [stream sediment archive](https://www.gsi.ie/data-and-maps/geochemistry/), release v1.1. Use the <150 µm fraction and the six published XRF oxide mass percentages. Exclude results below the stated detection limit or at the upper reporting limit. GIS transforms Irish National Grid coordinates from EPSG:29903 into WGS84. Contains Irish Public Sector Data (Geological Survey Ireland) licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) licence.
- **Topography and geology.** GIS applies the same NASADEM 5 km window, 500 m bins, 95% valid posting threshold, and shared slope metrics as in the parent release. Terrain rows retain the source windows and hashes. GLiM supplies geology at its native 0.5° resolution. Missing map values stay null. SPICE uses the same 24 dated Earth reference times and geometry as the parent release. See [methods and source links](ML.md).

Rebuild with `python build_earth_expansion.py` after installing the repository requirements plus `openpyxl`, `pyproj`, `rasterio`, `pyshp`, `shapely`, and `spiceypy`. Source files are checksum-pinned. The GIS processing cache is in `work/earth`. The browser app rebuilds with `python build_app.py`.
